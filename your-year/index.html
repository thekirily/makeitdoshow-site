What’s inside

It was written down in grey.
Here it is again, in color.

Every year, the government’s price agents walked into grocery stores in dozens of American cities and wrote down what bread, eggs and coffee cost. Those numbers sat in grey tables for decades. The booklet sets them beside real color film from the time, and adds the fairer measure: how long it took to earn them.

56 minutes of work at the minimum wage bought a dozen eggs in 1953. Today, 35.

From the booklet’s paycheck page. The minimum covered only some jobs in 1953: a yardstick, not everybody’s pay. Sources are printed on pages 18–19.

Before you order

Can it go straight to her?

Yes. Enter her mailing address on the payment page. Shipping to any U.S. address is included. There is no gift wrap.

Do I need a PayPal account?

Usually not. The payment page opens on a card form, so most people pay by debit or credit card as a guest. PayPal decides case by case when it asks for an account; if you already have one, you can use it.

Will it arrive in time?

Allow about 2½ weeks. Our printer’s own estimate for U.S. Mail is 11–13 business days from order to door. If you need it sooner, write to us before you order.

What if the name is wrong?

The cover prints exactly as the preview above shows, so check the spelling there. If something arrives wrong because of us, write to chaddoto2@gmail.com and we will make it right.

Is this the same as the free sheet?

No. The free sheet is a page of prices to print at home. The booklet is twenty printed pages with film, names, recipes, the paycheck page and the pages left for her.

Which years can I choose?

Every year from 1930 to 1970. Years before 1938, when there was no federal minimum wage, show the prices without the minutes-of-work comparison.
