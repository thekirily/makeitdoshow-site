A memory deserves a source

The record behind
the everyday.

We separate what the records say, what the film shows, and what a family remembers.

Prices: a table, a year, a unit.

The year pages use the supplied transcription of federal tables. Each page names the series behind every row. Food covers 1930–1970; later pages are partial. Prices are U.S. averages, not a local receipt. Historical bread is per pound; milk is half a gallon delivered. Modern milk is the store-bought comparison.

The reference column uses the supplied 2025 BLS averages, with a separately dated 2026 postage rate and federal wage rate. We do not describe these as live “today” prices. The original downloadable sheets use rounded cents and say “today”; read that column as this dated reference set.

Minutes of work are a calculation, not a claim about every worker.

Price ÷ last federal hourly minimum effective in the year × 60. The federal law did not cover every worker; rates and coverage could change during a year. There is no federal-wage comparison before 1938. The calculation uses the unrounded price and rounds only the answer.

Names: the national annual file.

The top ten female and male names come from Social Security’s national birth-year data. The booklet includes counts. These are application records, not a complete census of every birth.

Film: real, dated, and selected.

The reused frames come from A Word to the Wives (1955) and Shopping Can Be Fun (1957), promotional films held in the Prelinger Archives. They show period color footage, not someone’s private family album. Dates stay with the images. Any recreation added to our work must be labelled as a recreation.
